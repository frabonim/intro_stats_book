Example 2.2.8. Comparing two generations.

From 1984 to 1985, the mean height of 15- to 18-year-old Chilean males was \(172.36\) cm with standard deviation \(6.34\) cm, so \(Y \sim N(172.36, 6.34)\text{.}\) Recall \(X \sim N(170, 6.28)\) for 2009–2010.
🔗
Compare a 2009 male of height \(160.58\) cm with a 1984 male of height \(162.85\) cm.
🔗
Solution.
\begin{equation*} z_x = \frac{160.58 - 170}{6.28} = -1.5 \qquad z_y = \frac{162.85 - 172.36}{6.34} = -1.5 \end{equation*}
Both fall exactly \(1.5\) standard deviations below their own means. Relative to their own generations, these two heights are equally short—even though the raw measurements differ by more than two centimeters.

Example 2.2.4. Weight change.

Some doctors believe a person can lose five pounds on average in a month by reducing fat intake and exercising. Let \(X\) be the weight lost in pounds in one month, with \(X \sim N(5, 2)\text{.}\) Interpret the \(z\)-scores for a person who lost \(10\) pounds and a person who gained three pounds.
🔗
Solution.
For a loss of \(10\) pounds, \(x = 10\text{:}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} z = \frac{10 - 5}{2} = 2.5 \end{equation*}
This person is \(2.5\) standard deviations to the right of the mean.
🔗
A gain of three pounds is a loss of \(-3\) pounds, so \(x = -3\text{:}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} z = \frac{-3 - 5}{2} = -4 \end{equation*}
This person is four standard deviations to the left of the mean—a genuinely unusual outcome.
